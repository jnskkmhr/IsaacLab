Particle-NeRD Methodology

Translation-invariant neural particle dynamics with contact-impulse rigid-body coupling

Executive summary

Particle-NeRD is a hybrid Lagrangian simulator for two-way interaction between deformable granular or continuum materials and articulated or free rigid bodies. The material is discretized into persistent Lagrangian particles whose positions and velocities are advanced at each time step by a translation-invariant neural operator, while contacting rigid bodies are advanced by a standard rigid-body dynamics solver. The neural operator combines local neighborhood aggregation, global attention over spatially localized latent slices, and a translation-invariant description of the particle cloud as a whole, so that one model applies to confined beds and to unconfined clouds. Rather than regressing contact forces directly with a neural network—which is prone to high-frequency noise and can violate action–reaction symmetry, unilateral non-negativity, or Coulomb friction limits—the neural model predicts only particle positions and velocities. An analytical contact solver then reconstructs the 6-DOF reaction force and torque on each rigid collider from the predicted particle kinematics, local packing state, and direction-dependent effective-mass scaling.

Section 1 introduces the coupled simulation and its module interfaces. Sections 2–6 describe Modules 1–5 in the same order as Figure 1: kinematic prediction, local interactions, neural motion update, contact impulse solution, and rigid-body integration. Section 7 describes the recurrent training objective.

1.2   Staggered two-way coupling execution order

Each physical time step \(\Delta t\) executes the five modules in a staggered sequence:

  1. Capture collider state: At the beginning of interval \(\Delta t\), record the current world poses \(\mathbf{T}_{\text{col}}^t\) and spatial velocities \(\mathbf{V}_{\text{col}}^t\) of all rigid colliders, together with the interval-start particle positions \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\).
  2. Evaluate neural particle update (Modules 1–3): Given the particle history \(\mathbf{X}_{\text{hist}}^t\) and collider states \((\mathbf{T}_{\text{col}}^t, \mathbf{V}_{\text{col}}^t)\), predict the end-of-step particle positions \(\mathbf{x}_i^{t+1}\) and velocities \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\). Update the history buffer \(\mathbf{X}_{\text{hist}}^{t+1}\) so that its finite difference \((\mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{prev}, i}^{t+1})/\Delta t\) equals \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\).
  3. Solve contact impulses (Module 4): Using the interval-start particle positions \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\) and the newly predicted particle velocities \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\), fit local velocity gradients and Wendland packing densities, compute packing- and direction-dependent particle mobilities, and solve for the contact impulses acting on each dynamic rigid collider. Accumulate the resulting world-frame forces \(\mathbf{F}_b\) and center-of-mass torques \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_b\).
  4. Advance rigid-body dynamics (Module 5): Apply the reconstructed material wrenches \((\mathbf{F}_b, \boldsymbol{\tau}_b)\) to the rigid-body solver over its substeps across interval \(\Delta t\) to obtain \((\mathbf{T}_{\text{col}}^{t+1}, \mathbf{V}_{\text{col}}^{t+1})\).

2 Module 1: Kinematic predictor

A physical law governing granular or continuum particles does not depend on where the coordinate origin is placed in space. If a neural network receives raw global coordinates \((x, y, z)\) as node features inside its multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), moving the particle bed to a new location or changing the dimensions of the containment tray places the inputs outside the training distribution. To guarantee strict spatial translation invariance, our feature pipeline excludes global positions from all node MLPs and represents all particle–particle and particle–collider interactions purely through relative displacement vectors and signed surface distances.

2.1   Semi-implicit kinematic predictor and position-free particle features

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 2.1
  • \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\): Index of a material particle, where \(N\) is the total number of particles.
  • \(H\): Number of retained position history frames (typically \(H = 3\)).
  • \(\Delta t\): Duration of one particle prediction time step (in \(\text{s}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^t, \mathbf{x}_i^{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t-2} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): World-frame positions of particle \(i\) at the current step \(t\) and the two preceding steps \(t-1\) and \(t-2\) (in \(\text{m}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{v}_i^t, \mathbf{v}_i^{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Historical finite-difference velocities of particle \(i\) over the two most recent intervals (in \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): World-frame gravitational acceleration vector, typically \((0, 0, -9.81)\text{ m/s}^2\).
  • \(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Ballistic predictor position (in \(\text{m}\)) and predictor velocity (in \(\text{m/s}\)) of particle \(i\) under gravity alone.
  • \(\mathbf{m}_{\text{mat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}\): Normalized material property vector (density \(\rho\), Young's modulus \(E\), Poisson's ratio \(\nu\), internal friction \(\mu_{\text{mat}}\), yield pressure \(p_y\), tensile yield ratio, von Mises yield stress \(\sigma_y\), hardening, dilatancy, viscosity \(\eta\), particle mass \(m_i\), and particle radius \(r_i\)).
  • \(\mathbf{f}_i^{\text{node}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{node}}}\): Position-free per-particle feature vector of dimension \(D_{\text{node}} = 3(H-1) + D_{\text{mat}} + 4\), where \(D_{\text{mat}}\) is the number of material descriptors.

Given the retained position history \((\mathbf{x}_i^{t-2}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_i^t)\) of particle \(i\), the historical velocities over the two preceding steps are computed by backward finite differences in Eq. (1):

\[ \mathbf{v}_i^{t-1} = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^{t-1} - \mathbf{x}_i^{t-2}}{\Delta t}, \qquad \mathbf{v}_i^t = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^t - \mathbf{x}_i^{t-1}}{\Delta t}. \](1)

Before evaluating spatial neighborhoods, a semi-implicit Euler (trapezoidal) kinematic predictor advances each particle under gravity alone in Eq. (2) to form the predictor velocity \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i\) and predictor position \(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i\):

\[ \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i = \mathbf{v}_i^t + \Delta t\,\mathbf{g}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i = \mathbf{x}_i^t + \frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\bigl(\mathbf{v}_i^t + \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i\bigr). \](2)

The predictor position \(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i\) and predictor velocity \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i\) from Eq. (2) are used to query spatial neighborhoods in Module 2. However, the per-particle feature vector \(\mathbf{f}_i^{\text{node}}\) passed into the token MLP uses the actual historical velocities \((\mathbf{v}_i^{t-1}, \mathbf{v}_i^t)\) from Eq. (1) rather than \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i\) so that a particle at rest (\(\mathbf{v}_i^t = \mathbf{0}\)) has an exact zero velocity input rather than an artificial downward velocity \(\Delta t\,\mathbf{g}\). With reference step scale \(\Delta t_{\text{ref}}\) and reference gravity magnitude \(g_{\text{ref}}\), \(\mathbf{f}_i^{\text{node}}\) is defined in Eq. (3) and omits the global position \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\):

\[ \mathbf{f}_i^{\text{node}} = \Bigl[\,\mathbf{v}_i^{t-1},\;\; \mathbf{v}_i^t,\;\; \mathbf{m}_{\text{mat}},\;\; \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\text{ref}}},\;\; \frac{\mathbf{g}}{g_{\text{ref}}}\,\Bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{node}}}. \](3)

2.2   Multi-scale smooth collider surface queries

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 2.2
  • \(b \in \{1, \dots, C\}\): Index of a rigid collider shape (dynamic body link or static containment wall).
  • \(\mathbf{p}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\mathbf{q}_b \in \mathbb{R}^4\): World-frame position and unit quaternion orientation of collider shape \(b\), obtained by composing the parent rigid body's pose with the shape's local offset transform.
  • \(\mathbf{v}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\boldsymbol{\omega}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\): World-frame linear velocity at \(\mathbf{p}_b\) and angular velocity of collider shape \(b\).
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^{\text{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Position of particle \(i\) expressed in the local coordinate frame of collider \(b\).
  • \(d_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}\): Signed distance from particle center \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\) to the surface of collider \(b\) (\(d_{ib} > 0\) outside the collider, \(d_{ib} < 0\) inside).
  • \(\mathbf{n}_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Outward unit normal vector on the surface of collider \(b\) in world coordinates (pointing toward an exterior particle).
  • \(\mathbf{s}_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Closest point on the surface of collider \(b\) to particle \(i\) in world coordinates.
  • \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{surf}, ib} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): World-frame velocity of collider \(b\) at surface point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\), and its velocity relative to particle \(i\).
  • \(r_i \in \mathbb{R}\): Radius of particle \(i\) (in \(\text{m}\)).
  • \(g_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}\): Radius-adjusted signed surface gap \(d_{ib} - r_i\) (in \(\text{m}\)).
  • \(\ell_s > 0\): Near-field collider support length (in \(\text{m}\)), proportional to the particle radius \(r_i\).
  • \(\alpha_{\text{far}} \ge 1\): Dimensionless long-range support multiplier that extends collider visibility across deep particle beds.
  • \(\lambda_{\text{gap}}, \lambda_{\text{inf}} \in [0, 1]\): Blending weights for the long-range gap and influence tails.
  • \(\tilde{g}_{ib} \in [-1, 1]\) and \(w_{ib} \in [0, 1]\): Smooth multi-scale normalized gap feature and smooth collider proximity influence weight.
  • \(\mu_b \ge 0\): Coulomb friction coefficient of collider \(b\).
  • \(\mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^8\): Eight-channel feature vector describing the interaction between particle \(i\) and collider \(b\).

Figure 3 distinguishes the particle center, collider shape origin, closest surface point, and radius-adjusted surface gap used below.

For each particle \(i\) and each collider \(b\), the particle position \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\) is transformed into the collider's local frame via \(\mathbf{x}_i^{\text{local}} = \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{q}_b)^\top (\mathbf{x}_i^t - \mathbf{p}_b)\). Analytical signed-distance functions or trilinearly interpolated 3D signed-distance fields return the signed distance \(d_{ib}\) and local outward gradient. To prevent gradient singularities during backpropagation through multi-step rollouts when a particle crosses a sharp geometric edge, corner, or symmetry axis, exterior corner distances \(\|\mathbf{q}_+\|\) are smoothed as \(\sqrt{\|\mathbf{q}_+\|^2 + \epsilon} - \sqrt{\epsilon}\) for a small regularization constant \(\epsilon > 0\).

Rotating the local normal back into the world frame yields the outward unit normal \(\mathbf{n}_{ib}\) and the closest surface point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib} = \mathbf{x}_i^t - d_{ib}\,\mathbf{n}_{ib}\). The collider's world velocity at \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\) and its velocity relative to particle \(i\) are given by Eq. (4):

\[ \mathbf{v}_{\text{surf}, ib} = \mathbf{v}_b + \boldsymbol{\omega}_b \times (\mathbf{s}_{ib} - \mathbf{p}_b), \qquad \mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{surf}, ib} - \mathbf{v}_i^t. \](4)

The direct collider features must distinguish a supporting floor at different distances from a material particle. With a hard-clamped gap \(\operatorname{clip}(s_{ib},-1,1)\) and cutoff influence \(\max(0,1-s_{ib}^+)\), all positive gaps beyond \(\ell_s\) produce the same encoded gap and zero influence. This removes distance sensitivity and the collider's weighted contribution through this feature path; it does not imply that every other network path loses boundary information. Equations (5)–(7) replace that cutoff with a near-field and a longer-range smooth component. Figure 4 shows how the longer-range component retains a varying gap feature and nonzero influence beyond the old threshold.

\[ s_{ib} = \frac{g_{ib}}{\ell_s} = \frac{d_{ib} - r_i}{\ell_s}, \qquad s_{ib}^+ = \max(s_{ib},\; 0), \](5)
\[ \tilde{g}_{ib} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{gap}})\,\tanh(s_{ib}) + \lambda_{\text{gap}}\,\tanh\!\left(\frac{s_{ib}}{\alpha_{\text{far}}}\right), \](6)
\[ w_{ib} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{inf}})\,\bigl(1 - \tanh^2(s_{ib}^+)\bigr) + \lambda_{\text{inf}}\,\left(1 - \tanh^2\!\left(\frac{s_{ib}^+}{\alpha_{\text{far}}}\right)\right). \](7)

The two-scale curve decays more slowly than a single-scale \(\tanh\) encoding, while retaining near-contact variation. This extends the range over which a particle receives a direct collider signal; it does not establish static equilibrium or prove that material drift is eliminated. At sufficiently large gaps, the smooth encoding also approaches saturation, and finite-precision arithmetic can make the remaining tail numerically zero.

Combining Eqs. (4) and (6), the resulting 8-component collider-pair feature vector is defined in Eq. (8):

\[ \mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}} = \bigl[\,\tilde{g}_{ib},\;\; \mathbf{n}_{ib}^\top,\;\; \mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^\top,\;\; \mu_b\,\bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^8. \](8)

3 Module 2: Local interactions

For each material particle \(i\), \(\mathbf{a}_i\) summarizes nearby material particles: their spatial offsets and velocities relative to particle \(i\). The vector \(\mathbf{b}_i\) summarizes nearby collider boundaries: contact gap, surface normal, surface velocity relative to the particle, and friction. Both are learned feature vectors attached to the same particle \(i\), not material-property vectors or additional physical particles. Boundary surfaces include interacting rigid bodies and static containment walls. This particle–particle / particle–boundary distinction follows the terminology of WorldParticle [Wang2026]; the present boundary branch uses collider surface queries rather than requiring sampled boundary particles.

Symbols defined and used in Section 3
  • \(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i, \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Kinematic predictor position and velocity of particle \(i\) from Subsection 2.1.
  • \(R_c > 0\): Spatial neighborhood radius for the continuous convolution, proportional to the particle radius \(r_i\).
  • \(\mathcal{N}(i) = \{j : \|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j - \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i\| < R_c\}\): Set of neighboring particles within radius \(R_c\) of particle \(i\), found via a GPU spatial hash grid.
  • \(\mathbf{d}_{ij} = \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j - \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(r_{ij} = \|\mathbf{d}_{ij}\|\): Relative displacement vector and Euclidean distance from particle \(i\) to neighbor \(j\).
  • \(q_{ij} = r_{ij} / R_c \in [0, 1)\): Normalized neighbor distance.
  • \(w_{\text{poly6}}(q_{ij}) = (1 - q_{ij}^2)^3\): Cubic radial window weight that decays smoothly to zero at \(q_{ij} = 1\).
  • \(\mathbf{u}_{ij} \in [-1, 1]^3\): Ball-to-cube mapped local offset vector that maps the spherical neighborhood of radius \(R_c\) onto a 3D trilinear filter grid of \(M_{\text{grid}}\) cells.
  • \(\mathbf{U}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{grid}} \times 4}\) and \(\mathbf{V}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{grid}} \times 9}\): Raw trilinear continuous convolution basis tensors for material-particle neighbors and collider boundary surfaces.
  • \(\mathbf{a}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\) and \(\mathbf{b}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\): Learned continuous convolution embeddings of dimension \(D_c\).
  • \(K_i = |\mathcal{N}(i)|\): Number of neighbors within \(R_c\) around particle \(i\) (capped at \(K_{\max}\)).
  • \(\phi_i \ge 0\): Normalized local packing fraction of particle \(i\), defined as \(\frac{1}{Z_\phi}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} w_{\text{poly6}}(q_{ij})\) with normalization constant \(Z_\phi\).
  • \(\dot{\phi}_i \in \mathbb{R}\): Scaled and clipped rate of the weighted neighbor count: positive for local compression and negative for dilation. The dot denotes the encoded rate, not the unscaled derivative of \(\phi_i\).
  • \(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, i} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Window-weighted mean relative velocity of neighboring particles around particle \(i\).
  • \(\mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}} \in \mathbb{R}^6\): Six-channel local packing state vector of particle \(i\).

While global slice attention captures long-range load transmission across the particle bed, short-range granular mechanics—such as inter-particle jamming, shear dilation, and boundary layer slip—depend on the exact 3D arrangement of immediate neighbors within a few particle radii. Module 2 captures this local structure using a 3D Continuous Convolution (ContinuousConv) evaluated on a GPU spatial hash grid, together with an explicit 6D local packing state.

For each neighbor \(j\in\mathcal{N}(i)\), the relative offset \(\mathbf{d}_{ij}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i\) defines the normalized distance \(q_{ij}=\|\mathbf{d}_{ij}\|/R_c\). The radial window \(w_{\mathrm{poly6}}(q_{ij})=(1-q_{ij}^2)^3\) assigns greater weight to nearby particles and vanishes at the neighborhood boundary. Its normalized sum gives the packing signal \(\phi_i\) in Eq. (9), a weighted-count estimate rather than an independently measured solid-volume fraction. Differentiating the window along the relative particle motion gives the compression-rate feature \(\dot\phi_i\). Approach increases the weight and produces a positive rate, whereas separation produces a negative rate. Uniform translation of the neighborhood produces zero rate because all relative velocities vanish.

\[ \phi_i = \frac{1}{Z_\phi} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} w_{\text{poly6}}(q_{ij}), \qquad \dot{\phi}_i = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\alpha_{\dot{\phi}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \frac{6}{R_c^2}(1 - q_{ij}^2)^2\,\mathbf{d}_{ij} \cdot (\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i - \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_j),\;\; -\dot{\phi}_{\max},\;\; \dot{\phi}_{\max}\right), \](9)

The same radial weights define the mean relative velocity \(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{rel},i}\) in Eq. (10). This vector describes the average motion of surrounding particles relative to particle \(i\), while \(\dot\phi_i\) measures their relative approach or separation. The two quantities convey distinct information: a symmetrically compressing neighborhood can have zero mean relative velocity and a positive compression rate. Equation (10) combines the normalized neighbor count, the two scalar quantities from Eq. (9), and the three mean-relative-velocity components into the six-channel vector \(\mathbf{p}_i^{\mathrm{pack}}\).

\[ \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, i} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} w_{\text{poly6}}(q_{ij})\,(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_j - \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i)}{\max\!\left(\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} w_{\text{poly6}}(q_{ij}),\;\; \epsilon_w\right)}, \qquad \mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}} = \Bigl[\,\frac{K_i}{K_{\mathrm{ref}}},\;\; \phi_i,\;\; \dot{\phi}_i,\;\; \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, i}^\top\,\Bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^6. \](10)

The current kernel normalizes count by \(K_{\mathrm{ref}}=32\) and the weighted count by \(Z_\phi=16\). The neighbor-search cap \(K_{\max}\) is a separate quantity. The raw weighted-count rate is multiplied by \(\alpha_{\dot\phi}=0.05\,\mathrm{s}\) and clipped to \([-5,5]\). Thus the six-channel vector contains two normalized counts, one normalized rate, and three relative-velocity components; it should not be read as six material parameters.

Module 3 uses \(\mathbf{p}_i^{\mathrm{pack}}\) in two places. In Subsection 4.1, the particle encoder concatenates it with the position-free particle features before constructing the initial token in Eq. (12). In Subsection 4.3, Eq. (18) concatenates the same vector directly into the motion-head input, preserving local packing and relative-motion information alongside the features processed by attention. Thus Eq. (9) supplies scalar features to Eq. (10), whose output conditions both token encoding and motion decoding. The contact impulse solver in Module 4 computes a separate Wendland-weighted packing signal in Eq. (24).

In parallel, each normalized relative offset \(\mathbf{s}_{ij} = \mathbf{d}_{ij} / R_c\) inside the unit ball is mapped to the unit cube \([-1, 1]^3\) via the radial ball-to-cube mapping \(\mathbf{u}_{ij} = \frac{\|\mathbf{s}_{ij}\|_2}{\|\mathbf{s}_{ij}\|_\infty}\,\mathbf{s}_{ij}\) and trilinearly splatted onto a 3D filter grid of \(M_{\text{grid}}\) cells with feature vector \([1,\; (\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_j - \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i)^\top] \in \mathbb{R}^4\) to form \(\mathbf{U}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{grid}} \times 4}\). An analogous trilinear splat of the collider-pair features \([1,\; \mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}}] \in \mathbb{R}^9\) from Eq. (8) along the inward normal offset \(-\tilde{g}_{ib}\,\mathbf{n}_{ib}\) forms the boundary basis \(\mathbf{V}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{grid}} \times 9}\). Each offset generally lies between filter-grid nodes. Trilinear interpolation distributes its feature contribution among the eight surrounding nodes using products of one-dimensional linear weights, whose sum is one before application of the radial window. The accumulated grid features are equivalent to evaluating an interpolated spatial filter at each neighbor offset and summing the resulting contributions. The filter bank is shared across query particles. Two learned linear layers project the flattened bases into \(D_c\)-dimensional embeddings in Eq. (11):

\[ \mathbf{a}_i = \mathbf{W}_a\,\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{U}_i) + \boldsymbol{\beta}_a \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}, \qquad \mathbf{b}_i = \mathbf{W}_b\,\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{V}_i) + \boldsymbol{\beta}_b \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}. \](11)

4 Module 3: Neural motion update

Module 3 combines the local continuous convolution features with global slice attention to predict residual position and velocity corrections for each particle. To preserve strict translation invariance while still allowing the global attention blocks to reason about 3D spatial geometry across the bed, all global coordinate dependencies are introduced exclusively through 3D Rotary Position Embeddings (3D-RoPE), whose inner products depend only on relative 3D coordinate differences. The cloud-context path of Subsection 4.4 enters at the token encoder and at the motion head and is not drawn.

  • \(\mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^8\) and \(w_{ib} \in [0, 1]\): Collider-pair feature vector and proximity influence weight between particle \(i\) and collider \(b\) (Subsection 2.2).
  • \(\mathbf{a}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\) and \(\mathbf{b}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\): Continuous convolution embeddings from Module 2.

  • \(\mathbf{e}_i^{\text{part}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\) and \(\mathbf{e}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\): MLP-encoded particle embedding and collider-pair embedding.
  • \(\mathbf{z}_i^{\text{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3D_c}\): Concatenated local representation (\([\,\mathbf{b}_i,\; \mathbf{a}_i,\; \mathbf{e}_i^{\text{part}} + \sum_b w_{ib}\mathbf{e}_{ib}^{\text{col}}\,]\)).
  • \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^D\): Initial latent token entering the first attention block.
  • \(\mathbf{c}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{ctx}}}\): Translation-invariant cloud descriptor of particle \(i\) (Subsection 4.4).
  • \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\text{in}}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\text{ctx}}\): Normalized two-layer GELU projection of the local representation to width \(D\), and the cloud-context encoder of Subsection 4.4.

(10) are concatenated and mapped to \(\mathbf{e}_i^{\text{part}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\) by a two-layer GELU MLP. Simultaneously, a shared two-layer GELU MLP maps each collider-pair vector \(\mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^8\) from Eq. (8) to \(\mathbf{e}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_c}\), and the influence-weighted sum over all \(C\) colliders using \(w_{ib}\) from Eq. (7) is added to \(\mathbf{e}_i^{\text{part}}\). Concatenating the boundary-interaction and particle-neighborhood embeddings from Eq. 4.2   Spatially localized slice assignment and 3D-RoPE attention

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 4.2
  • \(\ell \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}\): Index of a transformer block, where \(L\) is the total number of blocks.
  • \(H_a\) and \(W = D / H_a\): Number of attention heads and per-head channel width.
  • \(S\): Number of latent physics slices per attention head.
  • \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^D\): Particle token representation at the input of block \(\ell\).
  • \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i = \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Mean-centered particle predictor coordinate (strictly invariant to global translations).
  • \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\mathbf{r}) \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times W}\): 3D Rotary Position Embedding (3D-RoPE) orthogonal rotation matrix parameterized by 3D coordinate \(\mathbf{r} = (r_x, r_y, r_z)\), satisfying \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\mathbf{r}_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\mathbf{r}_2) = \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1)\).
  • \(\tau_h > 0\): Learned softmax temperature parameter for head \(h\).
  • \(\mathbf{u}_i \in [0,1]^3\): Cloud-normalized coordinate of particle \(i\), obtained by scaling \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\) componentwise by the extent of the cloud (translation- and scale-invariant).
  • \(\mathbf{a}_s \in [0,1]^3\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_s^{\text{proto}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Fixed anchor of slice \(s\) on a regular lattice in the unit cube, and the corresponding prototype position in the cloud (the anchor mapped back through the extent of the cloud).
  • \(\lambda_h > 0\): Learned spatial-locality strength of head \(h\).
  • \(A_{i,s}^{(h)} \in (0, 1)\): Soft assignment weight of particle \(i\) to slice \(s \in \{1, \dots, S\}\) in head \(h\), satisfying \(\sum_{s=1}^S A_{i,s}^{(h)} = 1\).
  • \(\mathbf{t}_s^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^W\) and \(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Aggregated feature token and 3D spatial centroid of slice \(s\) in head \(h\).
  • \(\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_s^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^W\): Slice token after slice-to-slice self-attention.
  • \(\mathbf{f}_i^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^W\): Projected feature of particle \(i\) in attention head \(h\), before slicing.
  • \(\mathbf{q}_s^{(h)},\mathbf{k}_s^{(h)},\mathbf{v}_s^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^W\): Query, key, and value of slice \(s\) in head \(h\), defined by Eq. (15).
  • \(\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i^{(h)},\hat{\mathbf{k}}_s^{(h)}\): RoPE-encoded query and key for particle-to-slice cross-attention.
  • \(B_{i,s}^{(h)}\): Cross-attention weight from particle \(i\) to slice \(s\), with \(\sum_s B_{i,s}^{(h)}=1\).
  • \(\mathbf{o}_i^{(h)}\in\mathbb R^W\): Per-head feature returned from slices to particle \(i\).
  • \(\hat{\mathbf h}_i\in\mathbb R^D\): Intermediate hidden state after the attention residual; \(\mathbf W_O\) and \(\mathbf b_O\) are the learned output projection and bias.

Computing full pairwise self-attention across \(N\) particles would require \(O(N^2)\) memory and compute. Instead, each block aggregates the \(N\) particles into \(S\) learned physics slices per head, performs \(O(S^2)\) attention among slices, and distributes the attended slice tokens back to the \(N\) particles in \(O(NS)\) time.

If slice assignment weights \(A_{i,s}^{(h)}\) are computed purely from position-free features \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)}\), particles at opposite ends of the bed that share similar velocities are collapsed into the same slice, causing all \(S\) slice centroids \(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)}\) to collapse toward the bed center \(\mathbf{0}\). To ensure that each slice represents a localized 3D region of the bed while preserving translation invariance, Eq. (13) combines two mechanisms. First, 3D Rotary Position Embeddings rotate the assignment query at the particle position \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\) and the slice key at a prototype position \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_s^{\text{proto}}\), so that the feature score depends on their relative offset. Second, a learned spatial-locality prior penalizes the squared distance between the cloud-normalized coordinate \(\mathbf{u}_i\) and the anchor \(\mathbf{a}_s\) of each slice. Both the prototypes and the normalized coordinates are computed from the cloud itself, so the assignment is invariant to global translation and adapts to the extent of the cloud:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{g}_i^{(h)} &= \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i)\,\mathbf{W}_{\text{geom}}^{(h)}\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)}), \qquad \mathbf{k}_{\text{slice},s}^{(h)} = \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_s^{\text{proto}})\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{w}_{\text{slice}, s}^{(h)}), \\ A_{i,s}^{(h)} &= \frac{\exp\!\bigl(\bigl(\mathbf{k}_{\text{slice},s}^{(h)} \cdot \mathbf{g}_i^{(h)}/\sqrt{W} - \lambda_h \|\mathbf{u}_i - \mathbf{a}_s\|_2^2\bigr) / \tau_h\bigr)}{\sum_{s'=1}^S \exp\!\bigl(\bigl(\mathbf{k}_{\text{slice},s'}^{(h)} \cdot \mathbf{g}_i^{(h)}/\sqrt{W} - \lambda_h \|\mathbf{u}_i - \mathbf{a}_{s'}\|_2^2\bigr) / \tau_h\bigr)}. \end{aligned} \](13)

A learned projection maps each particle token to a per-head feature \(\mathbf{f}_i^{(h)}=\mathbf{W}_F^{(h)}\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)})\in\mathbb{R}^W\). This projection reduces the channel width from \(D\) to \(W\) while retaining one feature vector for each of the \(N\) particles. Using the assignment weights \(A_{i,s}^{(h)}\) from Eq. (13), Eq. (14) then aggregates these particle features into \(S\) slice tokens \(\mathbf{t}_s^{(h)}\) and their spatial centroids \(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)}\). Thus \(\mathbf{f}_i^{(h)}\) is the feature before slicing, whereas \(\mathbf{t}_s^{(h)}\) is the aggregated feature after slicing:

\[ m_s^{(h)} = \sum_{i=1}^N A_{i,s}^{(h)}, \qquad \mathbf{t}_s^{(h)} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N A_{i,s}^{(h)}\,\mathbf{f}_i^{(h)}}{m_s^{(h)} + \epsilon_s}, \qquad \overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N A_{i,s}^{(h)}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i}{\max(m_s^{(h)},\; \epsilon_x)}. \](14)

Next, slice-to-slice self-attention is evaluated in Eqs. (15) and (16) using RMS-normalized queries and keys rotated by their 3D slice centroids \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)})\) from Eq. (14), so that the attention score between slice \(s_1\) and slice \(s_2\) depends on their relative 3D separation \(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{s_1}^{(h)} - \overline{\mathbf{x}}_{s_2}^{(h)}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{q}_s^{(h)} &= \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)})\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{W}_Q^{(h)} \mathbf{t}_s^{(h)}), \\ \mathbf{k}_s^{(h)} &= \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)})\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{W}_K^{(h)} \mathbf{t}_s^{(h)}), \\ \mathbf{v}_s^{(h)} &= \mathbf{W}_V^{(h)} \mathbf{t}_s^{(h)}, \end{aligned} \](15)
\[ \tilde{\mathbf{t}}_{s_1}^{(h)} = \sum_{s_2=1}^S \operatorname{softmax}_{s_2}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}_{s_1}^{(h)} \cdot \mathbf{k}_{s_2}^{(h)}}{\sqrt{W}}\right) \mathbf{v}_{s_2}^{(h)}. \](16)

The attended slice tokens \(\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_s^{(h)}\) are returned to the particles through the original slice assignments \(A_{i,s}^{(h)}\) and a second set of cross-attention weights \(B_{i,s}^{(h)}\). For cross-attention, define the particle query and slice key as

\[\begin{aligned}\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i^{(h)} &= \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i)\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{W}_{dQ}^{(h)}\mathbf{f}_i^{(h)}),\\\hat{\mathbf{k}}_s^{(h)} &= \boldsymbol{\Theta}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_s^{(h)})\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{W}_{dK}^{(h)}\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_s^{(h)}).\end{aligned}\]

The softmax in Eq. (17) runs over slices for each particle. The resulting per-head outputs \(\mathbf{o}_i^{(h)}\) are concatenated and projected back to width \(D\). An attention residual update and a feed-forward residual update then produce the next particle hidden state:

\[ \begin{aligned} B_{i,s}^{(h)} &= \operatorname{softmax}_s\!\left(\frac{\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i^{(h)}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}_s^{(h)}}{\sqrt W}\right),\\ \mathbf{o}_i^{(h)} &= \sum_{s=1}^S \bigl(A_{i,s}^{(h)}+B_{i,s}^{(h)}\bigr)\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_s^{(h)},\\ \hat{\mathbf{h}}_i &= \mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{W}_O\operatorname{concat}_{h=1}^{H_a}(\mathbf{o}_i^{(h)})+\mathbf{b}_O,\\ \mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell+1)} &= \hat{\mathbf{h}}_i+\operatorname{FFN}\!\left(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_i)\right). \end{aligned} \](17)

Repeating this block \(L\) times produces \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(L)}\), which enters the motion head in Eq. (18). Thus \(\mathbf{o}_i^{(h)}\) is the per-head contribution to the hidden-state update, rather than a separate physical prediction.

4.3   Gated predictor–corrector position and velocity update

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 4.3
  • \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^D\): Output token of particle \(i\) after the \(L\) slice-attention blocks.
  • \(\mathbf{z}_i^{\text{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2D_c + D}\) and \(\mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}} \in \mathbb{R}^6\): Local skip-connection representation (Subsection 4.1) and local packing state (Section 3).
  • \(\overline{\mathbf{f}}_i^{\text{col}} = \sum_{b=1}^C w_{ib}\,\mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}} \in \mathbb{R}^8\): Smooth influence-weighted collider geometry skip feature.
  • \(\mathbf{u}_i^{\text{head}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D + 3D_c + 14}\): Combined feature vector entering the motion decoder MLP.
  • \(\gamma_{i,\text{inert}}, \gamma_{i,\text{col}} \in (0, 1)\): Sigmoid gates controlling the inertial velocity predictor and collider surface velocity predictor.
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Final predicted end-of-step position (in \(\text{m}\)) and velocity (in \(\text{m/s}\)) of particle \(i\).
  • \(\delta\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(\delta\mathbf v_i\in\mathbb R^3\): Learned position and velocity corrections after fixed output scaling, in metres and metres per second.

To preserve crisp local boundary geometry alongside the global slice-attention context, the output token \(\mathbf{h}_i^{(L)}\) is concatenated with the local representation \(\mathbf{z}_i^{\text{local}}\) from Eq. (12), the smooth influence-weighted collider geometry \(\overline{\mathbf{f}}_i^{\text{col}} = \sum_{b=1}^C w_{ib}\,\mathbf{f}_{ib}^{\text{col}}\), and the packing state \(\mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}}\) from Eq. (10) to form \(\mathbf{u}_i^{\text{head}}\) in Eq. (18):

\[ \mathbf{u}_i^{\text{head}} = \bigl[\,\mathbf{h}_i^{(L)},\;\; \mathbf{z}_i^{\text{local}},\;\; \overline{\mathbf{f}}_i^{\text{col}},\;\; \mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}}\,\bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{D + 3D_c + 14}. \](18)

The motion decoder maps Eq. (18) to a raw motion vector \(\mathbf m_i\in\mathbb R^8\) that holds a position correction \(\delta\mathbf x_i\), a velocity correction \(\delta\mathbf v_i\), and the logits of two sigmoid gates \(\gamma_{i,\mathrm{inert}}\) and \(\gamma_{i,\mathrm{col}}\); Subsection 4.4 adds a cloud-context contribution to \(\mathbf m_i\) before the gates and output scales are applied. Fixed output scales convert the two correction vectors to metres and metres per second. The velocity update in Eq. (20) combines the particle's current motion, the learned velocity correction, and the collider motion, with the inertial gate scaling the first two. Equation (19) integrates that predicted velocity and adds the gated position correction:

\[ \mathbf x_i^{t+1}=\mathbf x_i^t+\Delta t\,\mathbf v_i^{t+1}+\gamma_{i,\mathrm{inert}}\,\delta\mathbf x_i. \](19)
\[ \mathbf v_i^{t+1}=\gamma_{i,\mathrm{inert}}\bigl(\mathbf v_i^t+\delta\mathbf v_i\bigr)+\gamma_{i,\mathrm{col}}\sum_b w_{ib}\mathbf v_{\mathrm{surf},ib}. \](20)

The second term in Eq. (20) transfers the influence-weighted collider surface motion to the particle, scaled by the learned collider-motion gate. Here \(w_{ib}\) is the proximity influence from Eq. (7), and \(\mathbf v_{\mathrm{surf},ib}\) is the collider velocity at the queried surface point. A stationary collider contributes zero to this term. Because both learned corrections pass through the inertial gate, the gate scales the complete inertial-plus-learned motion of the particle, and the sigmoid bounds it strictly below one. The resulting particle velocity enters the position update in Eq. (19) and the contact impulse solver in Module 4.

To advance the history buffer \(\mathbf{X}_{\text{hist}}^{t+1}\) to step \(t+1\) while preserving the explicit velocity prediction \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\) from Eq. (20), the synthetic penultimate frame is constructed as \(\mathbf{x}_{\text{synth}, i}^t = \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} - \Delta t\,\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\), yielding \(\mathbf{X}_{\text{hist}, i}^{t+1} = (\mathbf{x}_i^{t-1},\; \mathbf{x}_{\text{synth}, i}^t,\; \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1})\).

4.4 Cloud-context conditioning and cylindrical skip decoding

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 4.4
  • \(\mathbf{c}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{ctx}}}\): Translation-invariant cloud descriptor of particle \(i\), computed from the predictor state of Module 1 and the collider queries of Subsection 2.2.
  • \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i,xy} \in \mathbb{R}^2\): Horizontal component of the mean-centered predictor coordinate \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\) of Subsection 4.2.
  • \(\mathbf{e}_i^{r}, \mathbf{e}_i^{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^2\): Radial and azimuthal unit vectors of particle \(i\) in the horizontal plane about the vertical axis through the cloud centroid; \(\epsilon_r > 0\) is a small regularization constant.
  • \(\mathbf{Q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Orthogonal map from (radial, azimuthal, vertical) components to world-frame components.
  • \(\mathbf{m}_i \in \mathbb{R}^8\): Raw motion vector: position correction (3), velocity correction (3), and the two gate logits (2).
  • \(\operatorname{Dec}(\cdot)\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\text{skip}}(\cdot)\): Motion decoder of Subsection 4.3 and the cloud skip decoder.

All coordinates entering Module 3 are relative, so the local representation and the slice attention alone give a particle limited information about the cloud it belongs to: its extent and aspect ratio, its clearance from the base and from the free surface, and the motion of the cloud as a whole. These quantities matter for unconfined clouds, whose shape changes during an impact, and for beds whose support depends on a boundary lying outside the local neighborhood. Module 3 therefore conditions every particle on a translation-invariant description of the cloud.

The descriptor \(\mathbf{c}_i\) collects five groups of quantities. (i) The particle position relative to the cloud centroid, normalized by robust, quantile-based horizontal and vertical extents of the cloud. (ii) The height of the particle above the lower extent of the cloud and its depth below the upper extent. (iii) Global shape features: the aspect ratios and absolute extents of the cloud. (iv) The velocity of the particle relative to the mean cloud velocity, resolved in a cylindrical frame about the vertical axis through the centroid, together with the mean cloud velocity. (v) Boundary clearances: the smallest floor gap of the particle, a lower quantile of that gap over the cloud, and the smallest wall gap over the cloud. No hand-designed gate switches the descriptor on or off for particular geometries; the encoder learns how strongly to use it. The descriptor is encoded by \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\text{ctx}}\) and added to the initial token in Eq. (12). The output layer of \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\text{ctx}}\) is initialized to zero, so training begins from the purely local-to-global model of Subsections 4.1–4.3 and the cloud context is introduced progressively.

For clouds that spread outward on a flat floor, motion has a natural radial structure. A second decoder therefore maps the descriptor and the local features of Eq. (18) to a raw motion vector expressed in radial, azimuthal, and vertical components. The unit vectors of the cylindrical frame follow from the horizontal offset of the particle from the cloud axis (Eq. (21)). Eq. (22) rotates the position- and velocity-correction components into the world frame and adds them to the output of the motion decoder; the gate logits are added unchanged. The final layer of the skip decoder is also initialized to zero.

\[ \mathbf{e}_i^{r} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i,xy}}{\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i,xy}\|_2 + \epsilon_r}, \qquad \mathbf{e}_i^{\theta} = \bigl(-e^{r}_{i,y},\; e^{r}_{i,x}\bigr), \qquad \mathbf{Q}_i = \begin{bmatrix} e^{r}_{i,x} & e^{\theta}_{i,x} & 0 \\ e^{r}_{i,y} & e^{\theta}_{i,y} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}. \](21)
\[ \mathbf{m}_i = \operatorname{Dec}\bigl(\mathbf{u}_i^{\text{head}}\bigr) + \operatorname{blkdiag}\bigl(\mathbf{Q}_i,\, \mathbf{Q}_i,\, \mathbf{I}_2\bigr)\,\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\text{skip}}\bigl(\mathbf{c}_i,\; \mathbf{z}_i^{\text{local}},\; \overline{\mathbf{f}}_i^{\text{col}},\; \mathbf{p}_i^{\text{pack}}\bigr). \](22)

5 Module 4: Contact impulse solver

Once Module 3 has predicted the end-of-step particle velocities \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\), Module 4 reconstructs the 6-DOF reaction force and torque exerted by the material on each dynamic rigid collider \(b\). Rather than using a neural force head, Module 4 solves a physical unilateral Coulomb contact problem between each rigid collider and the surrounding particles. Figure 7 summarizes the contact-to-wrench computation.

5.1   Surface velocity extrapolation and candidate contact selection

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 5.1
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^t \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\mathbf{v}_i^{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Saved interval-start position (in \(\text{m}\)) and neural predicted velocity (in \(\text{m/s}\)) of particle \(i\).
  • \(R_g > 0\): Neighborhood radius used to fit local velocity gradients and Wendland packing density.
  • \(q_{ij} = \|\mathbf{x}_j^t - \mathbf{x}_i^t\|_2 / R_g \in [0, 1)\): Normalized inter-particle distance within radius \(R_g\).
  • \(w_{\text{wend}}(q_{ij}) = (1 - q_{ij})^4 (1 + 4 q_{ij})\): Wendland \(C^2\) radial kernel weight.
  • \(\mathbf{G}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Regularized weighted least-squares spatial velocity gradient tensor \(\nabla \mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\) at particle \(i\) (in \(\text{s}^{-1}\)).
  • \(\phi_i^{\text{wend}} \ge 0\): Local Wendland packing sum around particle \(i\), defined as \(\sum_{j \neq i,\, q_{ij} < 1} w_{\text{wend}}(q_{ij})\).
  • \(d_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\mathbf{n}_{ib}^{\text{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\), and \(\mathbf{s}_{ib} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Margin-adjusted signed distance from \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\) to collider \(b\), outward unit normal on collider \(b\), and closest surface point on collider \(b\).
  • \(\mathbf{v}_i^{\text{surf}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Extrapolated material velocity at the collider surface point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\) (in \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{c}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\mathbf{u}_{b,\text{free}} = [\mathbf{v}_{b,\text{free}}^\top,\; \boldsymbol{\omega}_{b,\text{free}}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6\): World center of mass and contact-free 6-DOF velocity of rigid body \(b\).
  • \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^{\text{free}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Relative velocity of the collider surface with respect to the extrapolated material velocity prior to the contact impulse solve.

Because particle centers \(\mathbf{x}_i^t\) lie approximately one particle radius away from the collider boundary, Module 4 first fits a local spatial velocity gradient \(\mathbf{G}_i \approx \nabla \mathbf{v}_i^{t+1}\) and simultaneously accumulates the local Wendland \(C^2\) packing density \(\phi_i^{\text{wend}}\) over neighbors \(j\) within radius \(R_g\) via Eqs. (23) and (24):

\[ \mathbf{A}_i = \sum_{q_{ij} < 1} w_{\text{wend}}(q_{ij})\,(\mathbf{x}_j^t - \mathbf{x}_i^t)(\mathbf{x}_j^t - \mathbf{x}_i^t)^\top, \qquad \mathbf{B}_i = \sum_{q_{ij} < 1} w_{\text{wend}}(q_{ij})\,(\mathbf{x}_j^t - \mathbf{x}_i^t)(\mathbf{v}_j^{t+1} - \mathbf{v}_i^{t+1})^\top, \](23)
\[ \mathbf{G}_i = \mathbf{B}_i^\top \left(\mathbf{A}_i + \left(\frac{\lambda_{\text{reg}}}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}_i) + \epsilon_G\right)\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}, \qquad \phi_i^{\text{wend}} = \sum_{j \neq i,\, q_{ij} < 1} w_{\text{wend}}(q_{ij}). \](24)

Using \(\mathbf{G}_i\) from Eq. (24), the material velocity extrapolated to the closest point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\) on collider \(b\) is \(\mathbf{v}_i^{\text{surf}} = \mathbf{v}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{G}_i\,(\mathbf{s}_{ib} - \mathbf{x}_i^t)\). Let \(\mathbf{v}_{b,\text{surf}}^{\text{free}} = \mathbf{v}_{b,\text{free}} + \boldsymbol{\omega}_{b,\text{free}} \times (\mathbf{s}_{ib} - \mathbf{c}_b)\) denote the contact-free collider velocity at \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\), and let \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^{\text{free}} = \mathbf{v}_{b,\text{surf}}^{\text{free}} - \mathbf{v}_i^{\text{surf}}\). Particle \(i\) is selected as an active contact candidate for collider \(b\) if it satisfies the closing-gap condition in Eq. (25):

\[ d_{ib} \le \max\!\bigl(0,\;\; \mathbf{n}_{ib}^{\text{out}} \cdot \mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^{\text{free}}\bigr)\,\Delta t. \](25)

5.2   Packing-dependent effective mass and directional scaling

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 5.2
  • \(m_i > 0\): Physical mass of a single material particle \(i\) (in \(\text{kg}\)).
  • \(\phi_i^{\text{wend}} \ge 0\) and \(\phi_{\text{ref}} > 0\): Local Wendland packing sum of particle \(i\) (from Subsection 5.1) and the nominal reference packing sum of a settled bed.
  • \(\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i} \ge 0\): Local volumetric compression rate over step \(\Delta t\) (dimensionless), computed from the trace of \(\mathbf{G}_i\).
  • \(s_0 > 0\): Baseline mobilized column mass scale at nominal settled packing \(\phi_i^{\text{wend}} = \phi_{\text{ref}}\).
  • \(s_{\text{pack}, i} \ge 1\): Dimensionless packing-state mobilized mass multiplier for particle \(i\).
  • \(\mathbf{n}_{ib} = -\mathbf{n}_{ib}^{\text{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Inward unit normal pointing from particle \(i\) into collider \(b\) (the direction in which the material pushes on the collider).
  • \(\hat{\mathbf{g}} = (0, 0, -1)^\top\): Unit vector pointing in the direction of gravity.
  • \(\cos\beta_{ib} = -\mathbf{n}_{ib} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{g}} = n_{ib,z} \in [-1, 1]\): Cosine of the collider surface orientation angle \(\beta_{ib}\) relative to gravity (\(+1\) for a downward-facing bottom surface, \(0\) for a vertical side wall, \(-1\) for an upward-facing top surface).
  • \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, ib} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^{\text{free}} / \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}, ib}^{\text{free}}\|_2 \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Unit vector of the relative intrusion velocity of the collider surface with respect to the material.
  • \(\sin\gamma_{ib} = -\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, ib} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{g}} = -\hat{v}_{\text{rel}, ib, z} \in [-1, 1]\): Sine of the velocity dive angle \(\gamma_{ib}\) relative to gravity (\(+1\) when plunging straight downward, \(0\) when moving horizontally, \(-1\) when extracting upward).
  • \(c_{\text{attack}, ib} = \max(0,\; \mathbf{n}_{ib} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{rel}, ib}) \in [0, 1]\): Cosine of the local attack angle between the surface normal and relative motion.
  • \(\mu_b \ge 0\) and \(K_p(\mu_b) \ge 1\): Coulomb friction coefficient and Rankine passive earth-pressure coefficient of the material interface.
  • \(\alpha_n(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib}) > 0\) and \(\alpha_t(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib}) > 0\): Dimensionless normal and tangential multipliers for direction-dependent effective contact mass.
  • \(m_{\text{eff}, n, ib}, m_{\text{eff}, t, ib} \ge m_i\): Effective normal and tangential mobilized masses of particle \(i\) during contact with collider \(b\) (in \(\text{kg}\)).

When a rigid body contacts a granular bed, a single surface particle \(i\) does not react as an isolated mass \(m_i\) floating in empty space; instead, it is backed by a load-bearing chain of neighboring particles whose effective inertia depends on two physical factors: (1) how densely condensed the surrounding material is, and (2) the orientation and motion direction of the contacting surface relative to gravity and the free surface.

1. Packing-state mobilized mass \(s_{\text{pack}, i}\): Using the local Wendland packing sum \(\phi_i^{\text{wend}}\) from Eq. (24) and the local volumetric compression rate \(\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i} = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(-\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{G}_i)\,\Delta t,\; 0,\; \dot{\epsilon}_{\max}\bigr)\), the excess mobilized mass multiplier is given by Eq. (26):

\[ s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1 = s_0 \left(\frac{\phi_i^{\text{wend}}}{\phi_{\text{ref}}}\right)^2 \bigl(1 + c_\epsilon\,\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i}\bigr). \](26)

This formulation in Eq. (26) satisfies two essential physical limits: when a particle is isolated or airborne (\(\phi_i^{\text{wend}} = 0\)), \(s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1 = 0\) so its effective mass equals its exact physical mass \(1.0\,m_i\) and it moves freely; when the material is condensed (\(\phi_i^{\text{wend}} > \phi_{\text{ref}}\)) and actively compressing (\(\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i} > 0\)), the mobilized mass grows quadratically with packing density.

2. Direction-dependent effective-mass scaling \(\alpha(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib})\): Rather than assigning separate hard-coded mass multipliers to discrete collider faces, Eqs. (27)–(29) modulate the excess mobilized mass continuously as a function of the surface orientation cosine \(\cos\beta_{ib} = n_{ib,z}\), the velocity dive sine \(\sin\gamma_{ib} = -\hat{v}_{\text{rel}, ib, z}\), and the attack cosine \(c_{\text{attack}, ib}\), parameterized by the Rankine passive earth-pressure ratio \(K_p(\mu_b)\):

\[ \sin\phi_\mu = \frac{\mu_b}{\sqrt{1 + \mu_b^2}}, \qquad K_p(\mu_b) = \frac{1 + \sin\phi_\mu}{1 - \sin\phi_\mu}, \qquad \eta_{z, ib} = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\cos\beta_{ib} + \sin\gamma_{ib}\bigr) \in [-1, 1], \](27)
\[ \alpha_n(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib}) = K_p(\mu_b)^{\,\eta_{z, ib}}\,\Bigl(c_1 + (1 - c_1)\,\max(\cos\beta_{ib}, 0)^2\,K_p(\mu_b)\Bigr)\,\bigl(0.5 + 0.5\,c_{\text{attack}, ib}\bigr), \](28)
\[ \alpha_t(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib}) = K_p(\mu_b)^{\,\max(\cos\beta_{ib}, 0)}. \](29)

These directional factors scale the effective material mass used in the contact impulse solve. They do not directly prescribe a boundary force: the force and torque are obtained from the solved contact impulses in Subsection 5.3.

Combining the packing-state multiplier from Eq. (26) and the direction-dependent effective-mass multipliers from Eqs. (28) and (29) gives the effective normal and tangential mobilized masses \(m_{\text{eff}, n, ib}\) and \(m_{\text{eff}, t, ib}\) in Eq. (30):

\[ m_{\text{eff}, n, ib} = m_i\,\Bigl(1 + (s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1)\,\alpha_n(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib})\Bigr), \qquad m_{\text{eff}, t, ib} = m_i\,\Bigl(1 + (s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1)\,\alpha_t(\beta_{ib}, \gamma_{ib})\Bigr). \](30)

5.3   Coupled unilateral Coulomb impulse solve and rigid-body wrench accumulation

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 5.3
  • \(\mathbf{n}_{ib}, \mathbf{t}_{ib, 1}, \mathbf{t}_{ib, 2} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Orthonormal contact frame at contact point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\), where \(\mathbf{n}_{ib} = -\mathbf{n}_{ib}^{\text{out}}\) is the inward normal and \(\mathbf{t}_{ib,1}, \mathbf{t}_{ib,2}\) span the tangent plane.
  • \(\mathbf{R}_{ib} = [\mathbf{n}_{ib},\; \mathbf{t}_{ib,1},\; \mathbf{t}_{ib,2}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Rotation matrix mapping world vectors into the local contact frame.
  • \(\mathbf{r}_{ib} = \mathbf{s}_{ib} - \mathbf{c}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Moment arm from the rigid body's center of mass \(\mathbf{c}_b\) to the surface contact point \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\).
  • \(\mathbf{J}_{ib} = [\,\mathbf{R}_{ib},\;\; -\mathbf{R}_{ib}[\mathbf{r}_{ib}]_\times\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 6}\): Contact Jacobian mapping the rigid body's 6-DOF velocity \(\mathbf{u}_b = [\mathbf{v}_b^\top, \boldsymbol{\omega}_b^\top]^\top\) to the local 3D surface velocity at \(\mathbf{s}_{ib}\).
  • \(\mathbf{M}_b = \operatorname{diag}(M_b \mathbf{I}_3,\; \mathbf{I}_b^{\text{world}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}\): Spatial mass and inertia matrix of rigid body \(b\) about its center of mass.
  • \(\mathbf{C}_{ib}^{\text{mat}} = \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(m_{\text{eff}, n, ib}^{-1},\; m_{\text{eff}, t, ib}^{-1},\; m_{\text{eff}, t, ib}^{-1}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Local material mobility (inverse effective mass) matrix of particle \(i\) from Subsection 5.2 (in \(\text{kg}^{-1}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{K}_{ib} = \mathbf{J}_{ib} \mathbf{M}_b^{-1} \mathbf{J}_{ib}^\top + \mathbf{C}_{ib}^{\text{mat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Combined body-and-material Delassus compliance matrix at contact \(i\) (in \(\text{kg}^{-1}\)).
  • \(\beta_{\text{stab}} > 0\) and \(v_{\text{gap}, ib} \in \mathbb{R}\): Baumgarte penetration-recovery factor and target gap-closing speed (in \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • \(\mathbf{j}_{ib} = [j_{ib,n},\; j_{ib,t1},\; j_{ib,t2}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Unknown contact impulse in the local contact frame (in \(\text{N}\cdot\text{s}\)).
  • \(\omega_{\text{rel}} \in (0, 1]\): Under-relaxation factor for the parallel Jacobi contact solver.
  • \(\mathbf{F}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Reconstructed world-frame material reaction force (in \(\text{N}\)) and torque about \(\mathbf{c}_b\) (in \(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\)).

All active candidate contacts \(i \in \mathcal{C}_b\) on rigid body \(b\) are coupled through the rigid body's 6-DOF velocity \(\mathbf{u}_b = [\mathbf{v}_b^\top, \boldsymbol{\omega}_b^\top]^\top\) in Eq. (31):

\[ \mathbf{u}_b = \mathbf{u}_{b,\text{free}} + \mathbf{M}_b^{-1} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_b} \mathbf{J}_{ib}^\top \mathbf{j}_{ib}. \](31)

At each contact, the current relative velocity is \(\mathbf v_{\mathrm{rel},ib}^{\mathrm{local}}=\mathbf J_{ib}\mathbf u_b-\mathbf R_{ib}\mathbf v_i^{\mathrm{surf}}+\mathbf C_{ib}^{\mathrm{mat}}\mathbf j_{ib}\). The gap-recovery speed is \(v_{\mathrm{gap},ib}=(\max(d_{ib},0)+\beta_{\mathrm{stab}}\min(d_{ib},0))/\Delta t\). Define the residual \(\mathbf e_{ib}=\mathbf v_{\mathrm{rel},ib}^{\mathrm{local}}+v_{\mathrm{gap},ib}(1,0,0)^\top\). Equation (32) computes a scalar normal impulse increment followed by a two-component tangential impulse increment, with the other contacts held fixed during each local update. Subscripts \(n\) and \(t\) denote normal and tangential components:

\[ \begin{aligned} &\underset{\Delta j_{ib,n}\in\mathbb R}{\operatorname{minimize}} &&\frac12 K_{ib,nn}(\Delta j_{ib,n})^2+e_{ib,n}\Delta j_{ib,n},\\ &\text{subject to} &&j_{ib,n}+\Delta j_{ib,n}\geq0,\\[6pt] &\underset{\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}\in\mathbb R^2}{\operatorname{minimize}} &&\frac12\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}^{\top}\mathbf K_{ib,tt}\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}\\ &&&\quad+\bigl(\mathbf e_{ib,t}+\mathbf K_{ib,tn}\Delta j_{ib,n}\bigr)^\top\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t},\\ &\text{subject to} &&\|\mathbf j_{ib,t}+\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}\|_2 \leq\mu_b\bigl(j_{ib,n}+\Delta j_{ib,n}\bigr). \end{aligned} \](32)

The decision variables are the normal impulse increment \(\Delta j_{ib,n}\) and tangential impulse increment \(\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}\), both in \(\mathrm{N\,s}\); \(\mathbf j_{ib}\) denotes the current impulse. The normal problem is solved first, and its solution is held fixed in the tangential problem. Each quadratic objective combines the impulse-induced velocity response with the current relative motion. The normal objective includes gap recovery, and its constraint prevents attractive contact impulses. The tangential objective minimizes slip-related energy subject to the Coulomb friction bound.

The normal solve has the closed-form update \(\Delta j_{ib,n}=\max(0,j_{ib,n}-e_{ib,n}/K_{ib,nn})-j_{ib,n}\): without the constraint, \(K_{ib,nn}\Delta j_{ib,n}\) cancels the normal residual \(e_{ib,n}\). For the tangential solve, the implementation first solves the unconstrained \(2\times2\) linear system. If the resulting total tangential impulse satisfies the friction bound, it is accepted. Otherwise, a safeguarded Newton iteration with bisection fallback determines the nonnegative Lagrange multiplier that places the total impulse on the friction-disk boundary. Subtracting the current tangential impulse gives \(\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}\).

Contacts remain coupled through the shared body velocity in Eq. (31). Each Jacobi iteration applies \(\mathbf j_{ib}\leftarrow\mathbf j_{ib}+\omega_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Delta j_{ib,n},\Delta\mathbf j_{ib,t}^\top]^\top\) and updates that body velocity. Iteration stops at the residual tolerance or iteration limit. At a converged fixed point, the normal update satisfies unilateral complementarity and the tangential update satisfies maximum-dissipation Coulomb friction.

Upon convergence, rotating the local impulses back into the world frame via \(\mathbf{j}_{ib}^{\text{world}} = \mathbf{R}_{ib}^\top \mathbf{j}_{ib}\) and dividing by the step duration \(\Delta t\) yields the total material-on-body reaction force \(\mathbf{F}_b\) and center-of-mass torque \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_b\) in Eq. (33):

\[ \mathbf{F}_b = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_b} \mathbf{R}_{ib}^\top \mathbf{j}_{ib}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\tau}_b = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_b} (\mathbf{s}_{ib} - \mathbf{c}_b) \times \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{ib}^\top \mathbf{j}_{ib}\bigr). \](33)

6 Module 5: Rigid-body integration

The rigid-body solver receives the material-on-body force \(\mathbf{F}_b\) and torque \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_b\) about the body center of mass from Module 4. These wrenches act alongside gravity, joint drives, and rigid-to-rigid contacts. The material wrench is held over the rigid substeps of the coupled interval \(\Delta t\). Rigid integration advances the collider poses and velocities supplied to the next particle update, while Module 3 supplies the updated particle history. The staggered execution order is given in Subsection 1.2.

7 Recurrent multi-step training objective

Symbols defined and used in Section 7
  • \(K_{\text{hor}}\): Number of recurrent prediction steps in a training window.
  • \(k \in \{1, \dots, K_{\text{hor}}\}\): Step index within the recurrent rollout window.
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^{k}, \mathbf{v}_i^{k} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Autoregressively predicted particle position and velocity at rollout step \(k\).
  • \(\mathbf{x}_i^{k,*}, \mathbf{v}_i^{k,*} \in \mathbb{R}^3\): Ground-truth reference particle position and finite-difference velocity from MPM at rollout step \(k\), with \(\mathbf{x}_i^{0,*}\) denoting the initial position at the start of the window.
  • \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}, \mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}}\): Binary masks partitioning particle–step pairs \((k, i)\) into moving particles (\(\|\mathbf{x}_i^{k,*} - \mathbf{x}_i^{0,*}\|_2 > \delta_{\text{mov}}\)) and stationary particles (\(\|\mathbf{x}_i^{k,*} - \mathbf{x}_i^{0,*}\|_2 \le \delta_{\text{mov}}\)) for displacement threshold \(\delta_{\text{mov}} > 0\).
  • \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{rest}}\): Binary mask identifying particles whose instantaneous reference speed is below rest threshold \(v_{\text{rest}} > 0\) (\(\|\mathbf{v}_i^{k,*}\|_2 < v_{\text{rest}}\)).
  • \(w_{\text{pos}}, w_{\text{vel}}, w_{\text{rest}} > 0\): Loss weights for the balanced position loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}\), balanced velocity loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}}\), and stationary zero-drift regularization loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rest}}\).

The network is trained end-to-end on multi-step recurrent windows of length \(K_{\text{hor}}\) sampled from reference MPM trajectories of two kinds: interactions of prescribed or freely moving rigid bodies with a confined sand bed, and unconfined particle clouds released onto flat ground. The window length \(K_{\text{hor}}\) is increased in stages during training. Starting from an initial \(H\)-frame reference history at \(k = 0\), the model autoregressively predicts \((\mathbf{x}_i^k, \mathbf{v}_i^k)\) for \(k = 1, \dots, K_{\text{hor}}\) via Eqs. (19) and (20) using its own predicted particle histories without teacher forcing inside the window.

Because a large fraction of a granular bed remains stationary during localized collider interactions, a uniform mean-squared error across all \(N\) particles would be dominated by stationary particles. We therefore partition all \((k, i)\) pairs into a moving set \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}} = \{(k, i) : \|\mathbf{x}_i^{k,*} - \mathbf{x}_i^{0,*}\|_2 > \delta_{\text{mov}}\}\) and a stationary set \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}} = \mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}^c\), and weight both groups equally in the position loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}\) in Eq. (34) and velocity loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}}\) in Eq. (35):

\[ e_{\text{pos}}(k, i) = \left\|\frac{\mathbf{x}_i^k - \mathbf{x}_i^{k,*}}{\Delta t}\right\|_2^2, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}|}\sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}} e_{\text{pos}}(k, i) + \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}}|}\sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}}} e_{\text{pos}}(k, i)\right), \](34)
\[ e_{\text{vel}}(k, i) = \bigl\|\mathbf{v}_i^k - \mathbf{v}_i^{k,*}\bigr\|_2^2, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}|}\sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{mov}}} e_{\text{vel}}(k, i) + \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}}|}\sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{stat}}} e_{\text{vel}}(k, i)\right). \](35)

To prevent slow secular creep over long rollouts in static regions of the bed, an explicit step-to-step rest penalty \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rest}}\) in Eq. (36) penalizes finite-difference motion on particles that are at rest in the reference (\(\mathcal{M}_{\text{rest}} = \{(k,i) : \|\mathbf{v}_i^{k,*}\|_2 < v_{\text{rest}}\}\)):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{rest}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{rest}}|} \sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{rest}}} \left\|\frac{\mathbf{x}_i^k - \mathbf{x}_i^{k-1}}{\Delta t}\right\|_2^2, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = w_{\text{pos}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}} + w_{\text{vel}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}} + w_{\text{rest}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{rest}}. \](36)

7.1 Additional objective for unconfined particle clouds

Symbols defined and used in Subsection 7.1
  • \(\overline{\mathbf{x}}^{k}, \overline{\mathbf{x}}^{k,*}\) and \(\overline{\mathbf{v}}^{k}, \overline{\mathbf{v}}^{k,*}\): Centroid position and mean velocity of the predicted and reference cloud at rollout step \(k\).
  • \(r_i^{k}, z_i^{k}\): Horizontal distance of particle \(i\) from the vertical axis through the cloud centroid, and its vertical coordinate (reference values carry a star).
  • \(\Omega\): Finite set of projection angles in the \((r, z)\) half-plane; \(s_i^{k,\omega} = r_i^{k}\cos\omega + z_i^{k}\sin\omega\) is the projection of particle \(i\) at angle \(\omega\), and \(s_{(n)}^{k,\omega}\) is its \(n\)-th smallest value over the cloud.
  • \(\beta_n \ge 0\): Rank-dependent weights that emphasize the outer quantiles of the projected distribution.
  • \(\mathcal{K}_s\): Subset of rollout steps on which the pairwise terms are evaluated.
  • \(d_0 > 0\): Minimum particle separation, tied to the particle spacing; \(z_{\text{floor}}\) is the height of the floor and \(\epsilon_f \ge 0\) a small clearance.
  • \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{eq}}\): Particle–step pairs whose reference speed is below a small equilibrium threshold; \(\epsilon_c > 0\) is a small Charbonnier constant.
  • \(w_{\bullet} > 0\): Loss weights.

The particles of a freely falling cloud are exchangeable, and after impact the cloud spreads in a way that does not determine which particle goes where. A per-particle position error therefore penalizes an identity assignment that the reference does not fix, and it favors averaged, contracted clouds. For unconfined clouds the confined-bed objective of Eq. (36), denoted \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{bed}}\), is therefore supplemented by terms that act on the cloud as a whole (centroid motion), in a frame adapted to its geometry (cylindrical radial and vertical coordinates), and on the distribution of particles rather than on their identities (sliced Wasserstein and Chamfer terms). Further terms discourage clumping, penetration of the floor, and drift while the reference is in equilibrium.

Eq. (37) defines the centroid term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{com}}\) and the cylindrical term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyl}}\); an analogous term acts on the radial and vertical velocity components. Eq. (38) defines the sliced Wasserstein distance [Bonneel2015] between the predicted and reference distributions in the \((r, z)\) half-plane, evaluated from sorted one-dimensional projections, which is independent of particle identity.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\text{com}} &= \frac{1}{K_{\text{hor}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{hor}}}\left(\left\|\frac{\overline{\mathbf{x}}^{k} - \overline{\mathbf{x}}^{k,*}}{\Delta t}\right\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\overline{\mathbf{v}}^{k} - \overline{\mathbf{v}}^{k,*}\bigr\|_2^2\right),\\ \mathcal{L}_{\text{cyl}} &= \frac{1}{K_{\text{hor}} N}\sum_{k,i}\left[\left(\frac{r_i^{k} - r_i^{k,*}}{\Delta t}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{z_i^{k} - z_i^{k,*}}{\Delta t}\right)^{2}\right]. \end{aligned} \](37)
\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{sw}} = \frac{1}{K_{\text{hor}}\,|\Omega|}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{hor}}}\sum_{\omega \in \Omega}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta_n\left(\frac{s_{(n)}^{k,\omega} - s_{(n)}^{k,\omega,*}}{\Delta t}\right)^{2}. \](38)

Eq. (39) gives three pairwise or pointwise terms evaluated on the subset \(\mathcal{K}_s\) of rollout steps: a symmetric Chamfer distance [Fan2017] between the predicted and reference clouds, a short-range repulsion that penalizes particle pairs closer than \(d_0\), and a one-sided penalty on particles that approach the floor more closely than the clearance \(\epsilon_f\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ch}} &= \frac{1}{2|\mathcal{K}_s|}\sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_s}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\min_j \frac{\|\mathbf{x}_i^{k} - \mathbf{x}_j^{k,*}\|_2^2}{\Delta t^{2}} + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}\min_i \frac{\|\mathbf{x}_i^{k} - \mathbf{x}_j^{k,*}\|_2^2}{\Delta t^{2}}\right],\\ \mathcal{L}_{\text{rep}} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_s|}\sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_s}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sum_{j \ne i}\left(\frac{\max\bigl(0,\; d_0 - \|\mathbf{x}_i^{k} - \mathbf{x}_j^{k}\|_2\bigr)}{\Delta t}\right)^{2},\\ \mathcal{L}_{\text{floor}} &= \frac{1}{K_{\text{hor}} N}\sum_{k,i}\left(\frac{\max\bigl(0,\; z_{\text{floor}} + \epsilon_f - z_i^{k}\bigr)}{\Delta t}\right)^{2}. \end{aligned} \](39)

Finally, Eq. (40) extends the rest penalty of Eq. (36) to a finite-time arrest. For particles whose reference is in equilibrium it penalizes the squared finite-difference and predicted speeds, plus a Charbonnier (smoothed absolute-value) term that drives the speed to zero in finite time, as dry Coulomb friction does. Eq. (41) combines all terms.

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{eq}}|}\sum_{(k,i) \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{eq}}}\left[\sigma_i^{k} + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\sigma_i^{k} + \epsilon_c^{2}}\right], \qquad \sigma_i^{k} = \left\|\frac{\mathbf{x}_i^{k} - \mathbf{x}_i^{k-1}}{\Delta t}\right\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\mathbf{v}_i^{k}\bigr\|_2^2, \](40)
\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{cloud}} = w_{\text{bed}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{bed}} + w_{\text{com}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{com}} + w_{\text{cyl}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyl}} + w_{\text{sw}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{sw}} + w_{\text{ch}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ch}} + w_{\text{rep}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{rep}} + w_{\text{eq}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{eq}} + w_{\text{floor}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{floor}}. \](41)
